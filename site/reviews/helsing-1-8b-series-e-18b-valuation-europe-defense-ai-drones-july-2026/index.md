# Helsing Raises $1.8 Billion at $18B Valuation — Europe's Largest Defense AI Round Ever

> On July 13, 2026, Helsing closed a $1.8 billion Series E at an $18 billion valuation — the largest funding round ever for a European defense startup. The Munich-based AI company has been delivering autonomous HX-2 drones to Ukraine since late 2024 at a 70% mission success rate.


**At a glance:** Helsing is a German AI defense company building autonomous weapons platforms. Founded: 2021, Munich. Co-founders: [Torsten Reil](https://helsing.ai/company) (Co-CEO), [Gundbert Scherf](https://www.forbes.com/profile/gundbert-scherf/) (Co-CEO), Niklas Köhler (President). Round: [$1.8 billion Series E](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/13/helsing-fund-raise-defense-18-billion.html), closed July 13, 2026. Valuation: $18 billion post-money. Investors: Dragoneer, Lightspeed, Iconiq, Goldman Sachs Growth Equity, JPMorganChase, CPPIB, General Catalyst, Plural, Stepstone, Disruptive. Customers: Germany, Ukraine, Estonia. Key product: [HX-2](https://techstartups.com/2026/07/13/european-ai-defense-startup-helsing-raises-1-8-billion-at-18-billion-valuation/) autonomous attack drone (12kg, 100km range, ~€17,500/unit, 70% mission success rate in Ukraine). Three years after founding, Helsing has become Europe's most valuable defense startup. The company raised [$1.8 billion on July 13, 2026](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2026/07/13/helsing-raises-18-billion-in-europes-biggest-defense-startup-round/), in a Series E that Defense News called the largest funding round ever for a European defense-technology company. Investor demand reportedly exceeded available allocation.

The round values Helsing at $18 billion post-money — a figure that, [according to Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/madhulika-pathak/2026/07/14/helsing-cofounders-fortunes-get-big-boost-from-new-18-billion-valuation/), makes each of its three co-founders worth approximately $2.3 billion on paper.

---

## The Round

The Series E drew a mix of financial heavyweights and tech-focused growth funds. [Tech.eu reports](https://tech.eu/2026/07/13/european-defence-ai-leader-helsing-secures-18b-series-e-at-18b-valuation/) the investor list includes Dragoneer Investment Group, Lightspeed Venture Partners, Iconiq, Goldman Sachs Growth Equity, JPMorganChase, Canada Pension Plan Investment Board (CPPIB), General Catalyst, Plural, Stepstone, and Disruptive.

Notably, [Tech.eu confirmed](https://tech.eu/2026/07/13/european-defence-ai-leader-helsing-secures-18b-series-e-at-18b-valuation/) the company remains "predominantly European-owned" after the raise. That detail matters in the defense sector, where government customers scrutinize the nationality of controlling shareholders before awarding sensitive contracts.

The company did not disclose what it will use the funds for beyond its stated mission: accelerating development of AI platforms for [partner nations' defense capabilities](https://defence-industry.eu/helsing-raises-1-8-billion-to-accelerate-development-of-ai-enabled-defence-platforms-for-partner-nations/).

## What Helsing Builds

Helsing started as a software company. Its original product, the **Altra** platform, is described as an all-domain AI warfare operating system. [Altra](https://techbuzz.ai/articles/helsing-raises-1-8b-at-18b-valuation-in-europe-s-biggest-defense-ai-bet) combines on-edge AI with a degradation-resilient networking stack that processes sensor feeds from drones, radar, electronic warfare systems, and ground intelligence simultaneously, generating a real-time battlefield picture. It is designed to operate when communications are jammed — a critical requirement in modern combat where radio jamming is standard.

More recently, the company expanded into hardware with the **HX-2**, a small autonomous attack drone. [Tech Startups reports](https://techstartups.com/2026/07/13/european-ai-defense-startup-helsing-raises-1-8-billion-at-18-billion-valuation/) the HX-2 weighs about 12 kilograms, operates at ranges up to 100 kilometers, can identify targets autonomously, and continues its mission when communications are severed. Each unit costs approximately €17,500.

## Ukraine: Proving Ground

Helsing has been delivering HX-2 drones to Ukrainian forces since late 2024. As of January 2026, the company said it was shipping [several hundred units per month](https://ubn.news/helsing-produces-low-cost-ai-drones-for-ukraine-at-secret-german-factory/) from a factory in Germany — a factory described by reporting as operating under tight security. More than six Ukrainian army units had requested the system.

The company says the HX-2 has achieved a [70% mission success rate](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320370/20260713/helsing-closes-18b-round-reaching-18b-europes-defense-ai-giant-scales.htm) in live combat operations. For an autonomous system operating in a contested environment, that figure represents both a genuine capability claim and a liability — it means 30% of missions fail, which matters at scale.

Helsing's other customers are Germany (its home market, where it holds defense ministry contracts) and Estonia, one of the NATO states most directly exposed to Russian military pressure.

## The Founding Team

Helsing was founded in 2021 by three people who knew each other from European defense and technology circles. Torsten Reil and Gundbert Scherf are co-CEOs; Niklas Köhler is President.

Scherf's background is distinctive: before Helsing, he was a partner at McKinsey focusing on aerospace and defense, and from 2014 to 2016, he served as a [special representative in the German Federal Ministry of Defence](https://de.linkedin.com/in/gscherf). That combination of industry consulting and government experience helps explain how a three-year-old startup secured active military contracts.

## European Defense AI in Context

Helsing exists in a specific political moment: European governments, spooked by the war in Ukraine and questions about US security commitments, are accelerating domestic defense investment. [TechBuzz.ai notes](https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/helsing-raises-1-8b-at-18b-valuation-in-europe-s-biggest-defense-ai-bet) that investor demand for the round "significantly exceeded" available allocation — a sign of genuine institutional appetite for defense AI exposure, not just loyalty to the company.

The $18 billion valuation puts Helsing in the same bracket as many established European defense primes. It got there in three years and is still private.

## What to Watch

The HX-2's 70% mission success rate is an important number to track. As autonomous lethal systems accumulate operational data, that rate will either hold or shift — and its trajectory will shape how seriously European militaries commit to AI-native weapons platforms versus traditional manned systems.

The company's "predominantly European-owned" emphasis is also worth watching. It signals that Helsing is playing for government contract eligibility across multiple European defense procurement systems, not just German ones. As NATO members increase defense spending, a company with this ownership structure and these contracts is positioned to follow the money across multiple markets.
