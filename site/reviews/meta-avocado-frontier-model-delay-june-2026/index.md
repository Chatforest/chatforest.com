# Meta's Avocado Problem: A $135 Billion AI Bet That Keeps Missing Its Deadline

> Meta's next-generation frontier model, codenamed Avocado, has slipped again — from end-2025 to March to May, now confirmed for June at the earliest. The story of why reveals the widening gap between AI's haves and have-nots.


In January 2025, Mark Zuckerberg predicted Meta would soon have an AI system that could ["effectively be a sort of midlevel engineer" writing code](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ai-write-code-zuckerberg-says-164519848.html) for the company. Eighteen months later, Meta's flagship frontier model has [missed three deadlines and now won't arrive until at least June 2026](https://mlq.ai/news/meta-postpones-avocado-ai-model-launch-to-may-amid-performance-gaps-with-competitors/), a slip [independently confirmed by multiple reports](https://faq.com.tw/en/ai-ml/2026-05-19-meta-avocado-delay-closed-source-pivot-en/) in the days before this piece published.

The model's codename is Avocado. It is the most consequential unreleased AI system in the industry — not because of what it can do, but because of what its delays reveal about where Meta stands in the race it has spent $135 billion trying to win.

## The Delay History

Avocado was originally scheduled for release before the end of 2025, [according to multiple reports](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/03/13/meta-delays-avocado-ai-model-after-failing-to-match-rivals-xcxwbn/) on the project's internal timeline. It slipped.

The model resurfaced on analyst roadmaps for [March 2026](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/03/13/meta-delays-avocado-ai-model-after-failing-to-match-rivals-xcxwbn/). Internal performance reviews showed Avocado outperforming Meta's previous Llama-based systems — but not the competition that mattered. [The March launch became a May launch](https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/meta-s-avocado-ai-model-delayed-as-internal-tensions-rise).

In May, [sources with knowledge of the project told reporters](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/03/13/meta-delays-avocado-ai-model-after-failing-to-match-rivals-xcxwbn/) the model's benchmark scores still [fell between Google's Gemini 2.5 and Gemini 3.0](https://www.trendingtopics.eu/meta-delays-avocado-ai-model-again-might-even-license-gemini-from-google/) — short of the frontier tier Meta needed. By late May, [reports indicated a June launch window](https://faq.com.tw/en/ai-ml/2026-05-19-meta-avocado-delay-closed-source-pivot-en/) had replaced the May target.

That is three deadline slips. Each time, the stated reason is the same: the model is not yet at frontier performance.

## The Performance Gap

Understanding the delay requires understanding what "frontier" means in May 2026.

Avocado is reportedly *better* than its own predecessors — meaningfully so. It [outperforms Llama 4 and reliably beats Google's Gemini 2.5](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/03/13/meta-delays-avocado-ai-model-after-failing-to-match-rivals-xcxwbn/) on reasoning and coding tasks. For most purposes, that is an impressive system. (Meta's prior open-weight generation is Llama 4, released April 2025 — [Meta never shipped a "Llama 3.5"](https://ai.meta.com/blog/llama-4-multimodal-intelligence/).)

The problem is the goalposts have moved. Google's Gemini 3.0, OpenAI's GPT-5.4 and GPT-5.5, and Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.7 are the newer systems enterprise customers are evaluating. On the benchmarks that matter for that market, [Avocado has consistently landed short](https://faq.com.tw/en/ai-ml/2026-05-19-meta-avocado-delay-closed-source-pivot-en/) — trailing rivals specifically in [logical reasoning, software development, and agentic behavior](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/03/13/meta-delays-avocado-ai-model-after-failing-to-match-rivals-xcxwbn/).

Meta's leadership apparently saw this clearly: one internal option [Meta's AI leadership discussed](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/meta-avocado-delay-puts-135-billion-dollar-ai-bet-under-scrutiny/) was [temporarily licensing Gemini from Google](https://www.trendingtopics.eu/meta-delays-avocado-ai-model-again-might-even-license-gemini-from-google/) to power Meta AI's consumer products while Avocado caught up — though as of these reports no final decision had been made. That Zuckerberg's team considered licensing a competitor's model to fill the gap says something unambiguous about how they assess the current situation.

## The Talent Shift Behind the Numbers

The delay coincides with one of the most significant personnel transitions in Meta's AI history.

Yann LeCun — [one of three researchers awarded the 2018 Turing Award for pioneering modern deep learning](https://awards.acm.org/about/2018-turing), Meta's Chief AI Scientist for more than a decade, and the public intellectual most associated with the company's academic AI credibility — [announced his departure in November 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/19/meta-chief-ai-scientist-yann-lecun-is-leaving-the-company-.html) to start his own AI research company.

LeCun had publicly and repeatedly disagreed with the dominant approach to building AI, arguing that large language models trained on internet text would not achieve genuine intelligence. His departure signals that Meta's AI leadership has chosen a direction, and it is not his.

Meanwhile, [roughly 600 employees from Meta's FAIR unit](https://siliconangle.com/2025/10/22/meta-lays-off-600-ai-workers-looks-streamline-superintelligence-labs/) (Fundamental AI Research) — the academic-style lab LeCun built — [were laid off in October 2025](https://www.forbes.com/sites/zacharyfolk/2025/10/22/meta-laying-off-about-600-staff-at-ai-superintelligence-labs-heres-whats-impacted/) as the company reorganized around what Chief AI Officer Alexandr Wang called a leaner "superintelligence" push. Meta cut a further [roughly 8,000 jobs company-wide in May 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/meta-layoffs-may-2026-ai-restructuring-thousands), extending the restructuring beyond the AI org.

The restructuring is a bet: that the path to frontier AI runs through applied scaling and RLHF-style post-training, not through FAIR's more theoretical agenda. Whether that bet pays off is what Avocado will eventually answer.

## The Open Source Paradox

Meta's public AI identity has been built on Llama — the open-weight model series that the company has released to the public and that has become the de facto standard for self-hosted AI deployments.

Avocado is [closed-weight](https://faq.com.tw/en/ai-ml/2026-05-19-meta-avocado-delay-closed-source-pivot-en/) — [a break Meta has confirmed](https://thenextweb.com/news/the-unreleased-ai-metas-model) from its open-weight Llama line.

This is a deliberate break from Meta's strategy. The company has decided that its frontier model is too valuable — or too costly — to give away. Avocado will power Meta AI consumer products and enterprise API offerings; [reports indicate it will not be downloadable and self-hostable like Llama](https://cryptobriefing.com/meta-watermelon-ai-model-compute/).

The irony is significant. Meta earned enormous goodwill and developer trust through Llama's openness. Its frontier model is the one that stays behind a paywall.

For developers who have built workflows around open Llama weights, this means that Meta's best model — whenever it arrives — will be accessible only through Meta's API, at Meta's pricing, under Meta's terms.

## What Zuckerberg Bet

The broader context is $135 billion.

That is the upper end of [Meta's own 2026 capital expenditure guidance](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Results/default.aspx) — $115 billion to $135 billion, disclosed alongside Meta's Q4 2025 earnings and explicitly tied by the company to "increased investment to support our Meta Superintelligence Labs efforts." (Meta [raised that range further, to $125 billion–$145 billion, when it reported Q1 2026 results](https://fortune.com/2026/04/29/meta-zuckerberg-145-billion-ai-spending-roi/) in late April — spending is still climbing even as Avocado slips.) The money funds data centers, chips, power infrastructure, and the researchers and engineers who run them. It represents a complete reorganization of a company that, just three years ago, was primarily a social media advertising business.

Zuckerberg's public thesis: AI is the next computing platform, and missing it would be existential for Meta in the way missing mobile was existential for certain desktop-era companies. The investment is not optional; it is survival.

Meta's advertising business is still funding the buildout: the company [beat revenue and profit expectations in its Q1 2026 report](https://fortune.com/2026/04/29/meta-zuckerberg-145-billion-ai-spending-roi/) even as capex guidance rose — though investors sent the stock down roughly 7% on the same news, a sign of growing skepticism about the return on that spending.

Where the thesis is under stress is frontier models. The ad business does not require Avocado to be the world's best model. But Meta AI — the consumer chatbot that competes directly with ChatGPT and Claude — does. And that product is only as good as the model behind it.

## The Successor Is Already Named

In a detail that reveals something about how Meta plans long cycles, the company has already named Avocado's successor.

The next model is [codenamed Watermelon](https://cryptobriefing.com/meta-watermelon-ai-model-compute/).

Watermelon is intended to run on [Nvidia's next-generation Blackwell-Ultra chip infrastructure](https://markets.financialcontent.com/stocks/article/marketminute-2026-3-17-meta-hits-pause-on-avocado-ai-ambitions-face-seasonal-setback-as-execution-hurdles-mount) and will be substantially larger than Avocado — [reportedly using an order of magnitude more compute](https://cryptobriefing.com/meta-watermelon-ai-model-compute/). The engineers working on it are apparently doing so in parallel with Avocado's completion.

The implication: Meta views Avocado as a transitional step, not a destination. Whatever its performance level at launch, the plan is already for Watermelon to be where Meta competes at the frontier.

That may be the right strategy — long model development cycles mean the model you build today competes against models your rivals will release eighteen months from now, not the ones they have today. But it requires Avocado to be good enough to hold ground in the interim, and that is what three deadlines' worth of delays suggests has been difficult.

## What a June Launch Would Mean

If Avocado ships in June 2026 at something close to frontier performance, the delay narrative collapses quickly. A good model is a good model, regardless of when it arrived.

If it ships and falls short — if the June version is still a generation behind Gemini 3.0 and Claude Opus 4.7 — then Meta faces a harder question about what its $135 billion has purchased.

The AI industry is not patient with second place. Enterprise customers evaluating AI infrastructure do not hedge across providers the way they once distributed database contracts. The sales cycle for a major AI deployment tends to produce a winner and then inertia.

Meta's window is not indefinitely open.
