# How to (Actually) Buy SPCX: SpaceX's Unprecedented Retail IPO Allocation, Platform by Platform

> SpaceX targeted 30% of its IPO shares for retail investors but cut it to the low-20% range at pricing — still roughly double the historical norm. Here's how SPCX shares were distributed on Robinhood, Fidelity, Schwab, SoFi, and E*TRADE, and what actually happened.


**At a glance:** SpaceX priced its IPO at **$135/share on June 11, 2026**, confirming a **$1.75 trillion valuation** and a **$75 billion raise** — [the largest IPO in US history](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/12/spacex-ipo-spcx-live-updates.html), more than triple Alibaba's 2014 US listing. The [roadshow began June 4](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/05/17/spacex-accelerates-ipo-timeline-10-things-to-know/) (accelerated about a week from earlier estimates). [Trading opened on Nasdaq under **SPCX** on June 12](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/12/spacex-ipo-spcx-live-updates.html), and the stock closed its first day at **$161.11, up ~19%**. SpaceX CFO Bret Johnsen [had said](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/spacex-plans-outsized-retail-allocation-in-record-ipo-reuters-reports-4599593): *"Retail is going to be a critical part of this and a bigger part than any IPO in history."* SpaceX initially targeted a 30% retail allocation, but [cut that to the low-20% range shortly before pricing](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/11/spacex-cuts-retail-ipo-allocation-to-low-20percent-range-source-says.html) as institutional demand overwhelmed the deal. Shares were accessible through Robinhood, Fidelity, Schwab, SoFi, and E*TRADE — but demand still far exceeded supply, and most retail investors received only a partial fill or nothing.

---

The standard playbook for a large IPO reserves roughly **10% of shares for retail investors** ([Fidelity's own explainer on IPO share allocation](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/trading-investing/trading/ipo-share-allocation-process) describes the 90/10 institutional-to-retail split as standard). The other 90% typically goes to institutional clients of the underwriting syndicate — the hedge funds, pension funds, and sovereign wealth funds that the big banks cultivate over decades.

SpaceX set out to do something different.

In an April 6, 2026 call with its banking syndicate, [SpaceX CFO Bret Johnsen said](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/spacex-plans-outsized-retail-allocation-in-record-ipo-reuters-reports-4599593) the company planned an outsized retail tranche, calling it "a critical part" of the offering and a bigger share than any IPO before it. [CNBC reported in early June](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/09/spacex-ipo-explained-stock-price-date.html) that SpaceX was targeting a **30% retail allocation** — roughly triple the historical norm. Elon Musk has reportedly pushed for this structure, wanting everyday investors to participate at the same price and at the same time as institutional buyers.

According to a report cited by [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/1221350/spacex-ipo-heats-up-amid-unprecedented-retail-interest), one of SpaceX's lead underwriters told the banking syndicate the expected retail demand would be something they'd "never seen before."

**That 30% figure did not hold.** On pricing day, [CNBC reported that SpaceX cut the retail tranche to the "low 20% range"](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/11/spacex-cuts-retail-ipo-allocation-to-low-20percent-range-source-says.html) as institutional demand for the deal overwhelmed the offering. At roughly 20-22% of a $75 billion raise, that puts the retail-directed dollar amount at roughly **$15-16.5 billion** — lower than the ~$22.5 billion the original 30% target implied, but still far more than most entire IPOs raise in total.

What that means practically: **you can request shares. You are unlikely to get all of what you request.** Here's how to try, and what to expect.

## Why the Low-20% Retail Tranche Is Still Exceptional

For context on how unusual this is:

- **Typical large IPO**: 90% institutional, ~10% retail ([Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/trading-investing/trading/ipo-share-allocation-process))
- **Rivian (2021)**: Reserved [up to 7% for customers who had pre-ordered vehicles](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/11/10/rivian-pre-order-customers-made-over-240-million-in-profit-on-ipo-pop.html), plus [up to 0.5% via SoFi Securities](https://benzinga.com/news/21/11/23874991/want-to-buy-into-rivian-ipo-early-sofi-is-offering-early-access-to-customers)
- **SpaceX (2026)**: [Targeted 30% direct to retail brokerage platforms, then cut to the low-20% range at pricing](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/11/spacex-cuts-retail-ipo-allocation-to-low-20percent-range-source-says.html)

Fidelity's own investor education materials describe the 90/10 institutional-to-retail split as the standard. Even after the cut, SpaceX's final retail share was roughly double the norm — closer to a 4:1 institutional-to-retail split than the standard 9:1, though short of the roughly 2.3:1 ratio the original 30% target would have produced.

This is not charity. It reflects Musk's stated preference for broad individual ownership and, practically, an acknowledgment that retail demand for SpaceX will be unlike anything the market has processed. By routing it through established platforms rather than leaving it entirely to aftermarket buying, SpaceX can capture that demand at IPO price while giving retail investors the same terms as institutions.

## Platform by Platform

### Robinhood

Robinhood has established an [**IPO Access**](https://robinhood.com/us/en/ipo-access/) feature that lets users request shares in upcoming offerings directly inside the app.

For SPCX:
- Ensure **IPO Access** is enabled in your account settings
- Place a [**conditional offer to buy (COB)**](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/how-to-request-ipo-shares/) — you indicate how many shares you want; Robinhood says "each customer's eligible request has the same likelihood of receiving all, some, or none of the IPO shares they request"
- Robinhood's IPO Access program advertises [**no account minimums**](https://robinhood.com/us/en/ipo-access/)
- Any shares not allocated are returned to your buying power once allocation is finalized

Robinhood's COB process is designed for exactly this scenario: massive demand, limited shares, partial fills likely.

**What to expect:** Robinhood will likely see the highest volume of requests of any retail platform given its user base. Allocation per user may be very small.

---

### Fidelity

Fidelity offers [IPO access](https://www.fidelity.com/stock-trading/ipos) through its brokerage platform, with its own [share-allocation explainer](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/trading-investing/trading/ipo-share-allocation-process) describing how retail allocations typically work.

For SPCX:
- You need an active Fidelity brokerage account
- Indicate interest through the [IPO section of your account](https://www.fidelity.com/stock-trading/faqs-ipos)
- Notably, [Fidelity lowered its account minimum for IPO participation to $2,000, down from a prior $500,000 threshold](https://fortune.com/2026/06/08/retail-investors-participate-spacex-ipo-spcx-elon-musk-brokerages/) — a deliberate move to widen access for the SPCX deal
- Individual, joint, and IRA accounts are generally eligible for Fidelity's IPO offerings

Fidelity is one of [five brokerage platforms Fortune identified](https://fortune.com/2026/06/08/retail-investors-participate-spacex-ipo-spcx-elon-musk-brokerages/) as ways for retail investors to access SPCX: Robinhood, SoFi, E*TRADE, Fidelity, and Schwab.

---

### Charles Schwab

Schwab has [the most restrictive publicly disclosed access requirement](https://fortune.com/2026/06/08/retail-investors-participate-spacex-ipo-spcx-elon-musk-brokerages/) of the five retail platforms named for SPCX.

For SPCX:
- [Minimum $100,000 in eligible assets](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/how-to-invest-in-ipos-at-schwab) at Schwab, including individual accounts and IRAs, but **excluding 401(k)s**
- Must complete an [**eligibility questionnaire**](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/how-to-invest-in-ipos-at-schwab) and submit a Conditional Offer to Participate through Schwab's IPO Center
- Certain investment-objective and liquid-net-worth criteria also apply, per Schwab's own IPO guide

The $100,000 threshold is [Schwab's standard requirement for IPO participation](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/how-to-invest-in-ipos-at-schwab), not something unique to SpaceX. But it will effectively exclude a significant portion of retail investors from accessing SPCX through Schwab at IPO price.

If you are a Schwab client who meets the eligibility bar, check the IPO Center section of your account during the roadshow week.

---

### SoFi

SoFi [participated in the Rivian IPO in 2021](https://benzinga.com/news/21/11/23874991/want-to-buy-into-rivian-ipo-early-sofi-is-offering-early-access-to-customers), offering early customer access alongside Rivian's own pre-order-customer allocation.

For SPCX:
- Requires an active [**SoFi Self-Directed Invest**](https://support.sofi.com/hc/en-us/articles/360059036111-Am-I-eligible-for-IPO-investing) account (not a managed account); SoFi states there is no minimum balance requirement
- Complete an [**IPO suitability questionnaire**](https://support.sofi.com/hc/en-us/articles/360059049351-How-does-SoFi-s-IPO-Investing-work) before requesting shares — if the IPO isn't deemed suitable for you, SoFi lets you acknowledge that and invest anyway
- Submit an indication of interest once the IPO is live on the platform

SoFi says its questionnaire is meant to confirm you understand IPO-specific risks such as lock-up periods and the possibility of trading below the IPO price.

---

### E*TRADE (Morgan Stanley)

E*TRADE, now operating as part of Morgan Stanley, offers [IPO/new-issue access](https://us.etrade.com/what-we-offer/investment-choices/new-issues) through its standard process.

For SPCX:
- Active E*TRADE account required; per E*TRADE, [**employees and all other account types are not eligible**](https://us.etrade.com/what-we-offer/investment-choices/new-issues) — only individual, joint, and IRA accounts qualify
- Must complete E*TRADE's **Investor Profile questionnaire** to determine eligibility for a given offering
- Accounts must be **fully funded before the offering window closes**, per E*TRADE's own guidance
- Complete the IPO process through your E*TRADE account's IPO Access section

E*TRADE's participation in SPCX is notable because it connects to Morgan Stanley's underwriting syndicate role — [Morgan Stanley was one of the five active bookrunners](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/19/spacex-picks-goldman-sachs-to-lead-record-breaking-ipo-sources-say.html) alongside Goldman Sachs (lead-left), Bank of America, Citigroup, and JPMorgan.

## Realistic Expectations

Let's be direct about what is likely to happen.

**The demand math, updated with actual figures:** [Retail investors submitted more than $70 billion in orders](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/tomorrow-spacex-ipo-going-insane-141643376.html) ahead of the June 12 debut — against a retail tranche that ended up in the low-20% range of the $75 billion raise (roughly $15-16.5 billion available). That $70B+ in requests against ~$16B available implies a fill rate in the ballpark of 20-25 cents per requested dollar for a typical account, though actual allocations vary by platform and request size. Many accounts received a small partial fill or nothing.

**The practical outcome for most retail investors:** Most who didn't receive a full IPO allocation had to buy SPCX in the open market on June 12 at whatever price trading opened, not at the $135 IPO price. [SPCX opened at $150 and closed its first day at $161.11](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/12/spacex-ipo-spcx-live-updates.html), up ~19% — a smaller pop than Airbnb, which [closed its 2020 debut roughly 113% above its IPO price](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/airbnb-shares-open-for-trading-initial-public-offering-ipo-coronavirus-pandemic-183858304.html).

**The lock-up caveat:** If you did receive an IPO allocation, be aware that [Robinhood's IPO Access program enforces a "flipping" rule](https://yourway.blog/2025/09/05/how-ipo-access-works-on-robinhood-requests-random-allocation-flipping-rule-and-why-you-might-get-0-shares/): selling allocated shares within 30 days can result in being restricted from participating in upcoming IPOs for roughly 60 days. Check your specific platform's terms, since policies vary.

## What You're Actually Buying

A full breakdown of SpaceX's financials and the AI infrastructure angle is in our [S-1 deep-dive](/reviews/spacex-ipo-s1-ai-infrastructure-colossus-xai-anthropic-2026/) and our [SpaceXAI merger analysis](/reviews/spacexai-musk-merger-ipo-grok-orbital-data-centers-2026/). The short version for investors considering the IPO:

- **$1.75 trillion valuation** at roughly **94×** [SpaceX's actual trailing 2025 revenue of $18.67 billion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026036936/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm) (up from $14.0B in 2024)
- [Starlink (the "Connectivity" segment) is the only profitable segment](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/05/22/here-are-x-important-things-investors-learned-from/), posting a $4.42B operating profit in 2025; the Space segment ran a $657M operating loss and the AI segment (xAI, Grok, X) ran a $6.36B operating loss in 2025
- Elon Musk retains [**85.1% voting control**](https://investinglive.com/stocks/spacex-files-for-nasdaq-ipo-with-musk-retaining-851-voting-control-20260520/) via a super-voting Class B share class (10 votes/share) — SPCX shareholders have minimal governance rights
- The [AI segment (xAI, Grok, and X)](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-brief/xais-q1-2026-247b-operating-loss-on-818m-revenue-what-the-sp/) generated $818M in Q1 2026 revenue on a $2.47B operating loss; on a full-year basis, [the AI segment's $3.2B in 2025 revenue was well below Twitter's last full pre-Musk year (2022), when it generated roughly $4.4B](https://last10k.com/sec-filings/twtr/0001418091-22-000029.htm)
- The bull case includes continued Starlink subscriber growth ([12M+ customers across 160 countries as of mid-2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/spacex-starlink-surpasses-12m-customers-000951284.html)), orbital compute infrastructure, AI training revenue, and a Grok-driven advertising recovery — though analyst subscriber projections for 2030 vary widely and are not a confirmed SpaceX target

Buying SPCX is, in large part, a bet on Musk's long-term vision for orbital AI infrastructure — and on his continued operational involvement. The super-voting structure means that bet is not separable from the man.

## The Bottom Line

SpaceX targeted a 30% retail tranche and ultimately delivered a [low-20% retail allocation](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/11/spacex-cuts-retail-ipo-allocation-to-low-20percent-range-source-says.html) — still roughly double the historical 10% norm, and historically unprecedented in dollar terms. The five platforms that offered SPCX access were Robinhood, Fidelity, Schwab (for clients with $100K+ eligible assets), SoFi, and E*TRADE.

Most retail investors did not receive a full allocation — many got none at all, given [$70B+ in retail orders](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/tomorrow-spacex-ipo-going-insane-141643376.html) chasing roughly $15-16.5B in retail-tranche shares. [SPCX closed its first trading day at $161.11](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/12/spacex-ipo-spcx-live-updates.html), about 19% above the $135 IPO price, so the open market on June 12 turned out to be a more expensive entry point than the IPO price for those without an allocation.

What is not in question: even at a reduced allocation, SpaceX's retail structure was one of the most significant shifts in IPO access for individual investors among large-cap offerings. SpaceX CFO Bret Johnsen's promise — [retail bigger than any IPO in history](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/spacex-plans-outsized-retail-allocation-in-record-ipo-reuters-reports-4599593) — held up in absolute dollar terms even after the allocation was trimmed.
