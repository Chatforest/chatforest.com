# Meta Bets $100 Billion on AMD: The Deal That Made AI's Chip War a Fair Fight

> Meta's February 2026 multiyear deal with AMD — reported at up to $100 billion, 6 gigawatts of custom MI450-based AMD Instinct GPUs, and a warrant for 10% of AMD — is one of the largest AI hardware commitments on record, matched in scale only by AMD's own October 2025 OpenAI deal. It didn't go to Nvidia.


**At a glance:** [Meta + AMD](https://www.amd.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-2-24-amd-and-meta-announce-expanded-strategic-partnersh.html). Announced February 24, 2026. Reported at up to $100B over multiple years. 6 gigawatts of custom MI450-based AMD Instinct GPUs. 160 million AMD shares at $0.01. ---

On February 24, 2026, [AMD and Meta announced an expanded strategic partnership](https://www.amd.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-2-24-amd-and-meta-announce-expanded-strategic-partnersh.html) to deploy up to 6 gigawatts of AMD Instinct GPUs — a deal [TechCrunch and other outlets reported could be worth up to $100 billion](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/24/meta-strikes-up-to-100b-amd-chip-deal-as-it-chases-personal-superintelligence/) over multiple years, though [neither company has disclosed an official dollar figure](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/24/meta-to-use-6gw-of-amd-gpus-days-after-expanded-nvidia-ai-chip-deal.html). The first deployment phase will use a [custom AMD Instinct GPU based on the MI450 architecture](https://www.amd.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-2-24-amd-and-meta-announce-expanded-strategic-partnersh.html), not the "MI540" this piece originally (and incorrectly) reported — AMD's own announcement and [CNBC's reporting](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/24/meta-to-use-6gw-of-amd-gpus-days-after-expanded-nvidia-ai-chip-deal.html) both confirm MI450; no AMD product called "MI540" exists in the companies' materials.

What made headlines beyond the chip volume: [AMD issued Meta a performance-based warrant for up to 160 million shares](https://betanews.com/article/meta-100b-amd-ai-chip-deal-10-percent-stake-data-centers/) — [about 10 percent of the company](https://www.amd.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-2-24-amd-and-meta-announce-expanded-strategic-partnersh.html) — at an exercise price of one cent, vesting in tranches tied to GPU shipment milestones and AMD's stock crossing $600.

This is not a standard vendor relationship. Meta is effectively becoming an AMD co-investor as well as a customer — though [AMD struck a nearly identical warrant-for-chips structure with OpenAI in October 2025](https://www.amd.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-10-6-amd-and-openai-announce-strategic-partnership-to-d.html) for the same 6 gigawatts and the same 160-million-share warrant, so Meta is not uniquely AMD's largest customer by this measure.

---

## Why Meta Did This Now

The timing reflects two compounding pressures.

First, Nvidia's pricing power. By early 2026, [Nvidia controlled roughly 90% of the AI chip market and carried a $4.66 trillion valuation](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/24/meta-to-use-6gw-of-amd-gpus-days-after-expanded-nvidia-ai-chip-deal.html) versus AMD's $320 billion — a gap that had translated into sustained premium pricing and constrained allocation for buyers. Meta [projected up to $135 billion in capital expenditure for 2026 alone](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/24/meta-strikes-up-to-100b-amd-chip-deal-as-it-chases-personal-superintelligence/), and dependence on a single chip supplier at that spending scale created meaningful cost and supply risk.

Second, the "personal superintelligence" framing. [Zuckerberg said the AMD partnership is "an important step" as Meta diversifies its compute](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/24/meta-strikes-up-to-100b-amd-chip-deal-as-it-chases-personal-superintelligence/) toward delivering "personal superintelligence," which he has [defined as AI systems designed to deeply understand and empower individuals in their everyday lives](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/24/meta-strikes-up-to-100b-amd-chip-deal-as-it-chases-personal-superintelligence/). Achieving that requires a chip strategy, not just a chip order.

AMD's [custom MI450-based GPU, built on AMD's Helios rack-scale architecture and optimized specifically for Meta's workloads](https://www.amd.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-2-24-amd-and-meta-announce-expanded-strategic-partnersh.html), offered what earlier generations couldn't: a vendor willing to customize architecture around Meta's specific requirements at hyperscale.

## Deal Structure

**Volume commitment:** Up to 6 gigawatts of custom AMD Instinct MI450-based GPUs plus 6th-generation AMD EPYC ("Venice") CPUs. [Shipments supporting the first gigawatt of deployment are scheduled to begin in the second half of 2026](https://www.amd.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-2-24-amd-and-meta-announce-expanded-strategic-partnersh.html); neither company's announcement names a specific data center for this initial deployment.

**Warrant terms:** [AMD issued Meta a performance-based warrant for up to 160 million shares at $0.01 per share](https://www.amd.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-2-24-amd-and-meta-announce-expanded-strategic-partnersh.html) — roughly 10 percent equity stake — vesting in tranches tied to:
- [GPU shipment milestones, starting with the first tranche vesting at the initial 1-gigawatt shipment](https://betanews.com/article/meta-100b-amd-ai-chip-deal-10-percent-stake-data-centers/)
- AMD share price reaching $600 thresholds (AMD [closed at $196.60 the day before the announcement](https://betanews.com/article/meta-100b-amd-ai-chip-deal-10-percent-stake-data-centers/))

The warrant structure is unusual in a chip procurement deal. It aligns AMD's incentives with delivery execution and ties Meta's financial upside directly to AMD's success as a company. If AMD's stock climbs above $600, Meta holds a position worth billions at essentially no cost. The [warrant is exercisable until February 23, 2031](https://betanews.com/article/meta-100b-amd-ai-chip-deal-10-percent-stake-data-centers/).

## The Nvidia Context

The Meta-AMD deal was announced days after Meta separately committed to deploying [millions of Nvidia Blackwell and Rubin GPUs, alongside Grace and future Vera CPUs](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/meta-builds-ai-infrastructure-with-nvidia), in a [different multiyear partnership Nvidia and Meta confirmed on February 17, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/24/meta-to-use-6gw-of-amd-gpus-days-after-expanded-nvidia-ai-chip-deal.html).

Both deals existing simultaneously is the point.

Meta's strategy is explicit diversification — not an AMD bet against Nvidia, but a two-supplier architecture that reduces leverage on either side. The AMD warrant structure suggests Meta also wants AMD to succeed as a long-term viable alternative, creating sustained competitive pressure on Nvidia pricing.

For the broader AI chip market, the signal is significant: one of the world's largest AI buyers is deliberately cultivating AMD as a second-tier-one supplier, not just a fallback.

---

## AMD's Position

AMD entered 2026 as a distant but credible number-two in AI compute — [valued at $320 billion against Nvidia's roughly 90% market share and $4.66 trillion valuation](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/24/meta-to-use-6gw-of-amd-gpus-days-after-expanded-nvidia-ai-chip-deal.html) — having [already deployed AMD Instinct MI300 and MI350 series GPUs, plus millions of EPYC CPUs, across Meta's infrastructure in prior generations](https://www.amd.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-2-24-amd-and-meta-announce-expanded-strategic-partnersh.html). The Meta deal changes AMD's market position more structurally than any benchmark result could.

A committed customer at this scale — reported at up to $100 billion — means AMD can:
- Justify roadmap investment with certainty
- Build manufacturing volume with TSMC to reduce per-unit costs
- Signal to other hyperscalers that AMD can handle frontier-scale workloads

The 160-million-share warrant creates an unusual alignment dynamic: Meta is now financially incentivized for AMD's stock to appreciate, which means Meta has some interest in AMD performing well across all customers — not just in Meta's own deployments.

---

## Deployment Outlook

The six-gigawatt total represents a multi-year buildout. At full scale, [6 gigawatts of data center power demand is roughly equivalent to the electricity used by about 5 million U.S. households in a year](https://betanews.com/article/meta-100b-amd-ai-chip-deal-10-percent-stake-data-centers/). Neither AMD nor Meta has named which specific data center will house the first-phase deployment — this piece previously and incorrectly stated it was Meta's Louisiana Hyperion campus, but that facility is a [separate, unrelated Meta project](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/13/meta-louisiana-data-center-investment-reaches-50-billion-amid-ai-push.html) with no confirmed link to the AMD GPU order, so that claim has been removed.

As for how the AMD volume compares to Meta's other chip commitments: it's not clearly the largest. [AMD's October 2025 deal with OpenAI committed to the identical structure](https://www.amd.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-10-6-amd-and-openai-announce-strategic-partnership-to-d.html) — 6 gigawatts, a 160-million-share warrant, first shipments of MI450 GPUs in the second half of 2026 — meaning Meta and OpenAI currently hold comparably sized AMD commitments.

---

## What This Means

The Meta-AMD deal matters beyond the two companies involved.

It confirms that Nvidia's AI chip dominance, while real and durable in the near term, is no longer structurally unchallenged. When the world's largest AI infrastructure spender deliberately builds a two-supplier chip strategy and gives their second supplier a path to becoming partially owned in return, the dynamics shift.

AMD needed a customer willing to treat them as a tier-one partner at hyperscale. Meta needed a supplier it could influence structurally, not just purchase from. The February 2026 deal created both.

For AI infrastructure broadly: the chip arms race now has two serious combatants on the supply side. That's a different world than 2025.
