# June 2026 AI Events Calendar: GTC Taipei, Microsoft Build, WWDC, SpaceX IPO, and the Model Releases That Could Drop Any Day

> June 2026 is the most event-dense month in AI this year. GTC Taipei, Microsoft Build, WWDC, Code with Claude Tokyo, and the SpaceX IPO all land within two weeks. Here's your complete calendar with what to watch at each.


There are months in AI where nothing much happens, and there are months where five things land in two weeks. June 2026 is the second kind.

Starting May 31 (if you're in Pacific time) and running through June 12, the AI industry gets a Jensen Huang keynote, a Microsoft developer conference, Apple's most AI-loaded WWDC in memory, the SpaceX IPO, and an Anthropic developer event in Tokyo — all before mid-month. Meanwhile, Grok 5, GPT-5.6, and Gemini 3.5 Pro are each expected before July, and Anthropic's $900 billion funding round is expected to close any day.

This is the calendar, what's confirmed, and what to actually watch at each.

## Imminent: Anthropic's $900B Round

**Status: Not yet confirmed closed as of May 28**

[Bloomberg reported on May 22](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-22/anthropic-to-close-over-30-billion-round-as-soon-as-next-week) that Anthropic was set to close a round of over $30 billion at a post-money valuation above $900 billion — led by Sequoia, Dragoneer, Altimeter, and Greenoaks, each committing roughly $2 billion — as soon as the week of May 26. That week is now. No official press release has dropped yet, but the deal is expected to sign any day.

If it closes on reported terms, Anthropic will surpass OpenAI as the world's most valuable private AI company. It would also be Anthropic's second major round in a single calendar year, after [Bloomberg reported the Series G closed in February at a $380 billion valuation](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-12/anthropic-finalizes-30-billion-funding-at-380-billion-value) (also a $30 billion raise).

**Update (checked 2026-07-29):** the round closed on May 28 — but at materially larger terms than reported here. Per [Anthropic's own announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h), the company raised **$65 billion** in Series H funding (not $30 billion) at a **$965 billion** post-money valuation (not $900 billion), still led by Altimeter, Dragoneer, Greenoaks, and Sequoia, with Capital Group, Coatue, D1 Capital, GIC, ICONIQ, and XN co-leading and roughly $15 billion rolled in from existing hyperscaler commitments including $5 billion from Amazon.

## June 1 — NVIDIA GTC Taipei

**Where:** Taipei Music Center, Taiwan
**Keynote time:** 11:00 a.m. Taipei Standard Time (May 31, 8:00 p.m. PT / 11:00 p.m. ET)
**Livestream:** [nvidia.com/en-tw/gtc/taipei/keynote](https://www.nvidia.com/en-tw/gtc/taipei/keynote/)

Jensen Huang keynotes GTC Taipei one day before Computex opens. This is the hardware developer conference for the AI era, and the June 1 slot typically carries the biggest formal announcements.

**What's expected (pre-event reporting, unconfirmed until the keynote):**

- **N1X formal reveal.** NVIDIA's ARM-based AI PC SoC, [developed with MediaTek](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/cpus/nvidia-and-mediatek-may-unveil-jointly-developed-n1-arm-chips-for-windows-pcs-at-computex), is rumored to debut around this week — [reported specs include a 20-core Arm CPU with an integrated Blackwell-architecture GPU](https://www.pcworld.com/article/3151058/nvidias-n1x-could-be-the-jolt-windows-laptops-need-with-one-big-catch.html), NVIDIA's first entry into the consumer laptop chip market. OEM designs from Dell, Lenovo, Acer, ASUS, and MSI are expected to accompany it, with Computex (June 2–4) as the more likely reveal stage if it doesn't land in the June 1 keynote itself.
- **Vera Rubin partner news.** Vera Rubin is NVIDIA's next-generation data center platform (successor to Blackwell) — [NVIDIA has described it as a "multi-rack, pod-scale system"](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/vera-rubin-full-production-agentic-ai-factory) combining Rubin GPUs, Vera CPUs, and NVLink. Expect rack-scale system design partner announcements.
- **Physical AI keynote thread.** NVIDIA has been positioning its stack around physical AI — Jetson Thor, Isaac, robotics infrastructure. Recent keynotes have leaned on an "AI factory" framing tying together energy, infrastructure, compute, models, and applications.
- **DLSS 5 rollout update.** DLSS 5 — [NVIDIA's neural-rendering system announced at GTC in March 2026](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-dlss-5-delivers-ai-powered-breakthrough-in-visual-fidelity-for-games), launching fall 2026 on RTX 50-series GPUs with Bethesda, Capcom, Ubisoft, and Warner Bros. Games among the launch partners — is expected to get an adoption-numbers and new-title update here.

**Update (checked 2026-07-29):** [SiliconANGLE's recap of the actual June 1 keynote](https://siliconangle.com/2026/06/01/five-thoughts-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huangs-gtc-taipei-2026-keynote/) confirms Huang did unveil Vera Rubin details — including an 88-core Vera CPU and a cable-free NVLink 72 rack design — and centered the keynote on "the age of agents" and AI-factory economics. That recap does not mention an N1X reveal in the keynote itself, so that announcement likely landed at Computex days later rather than in Huang's June 1 talk as originally expected.

GTC Taipei's main conference runs June 2–4 at the Taipei International Convention Center, alongside Computex.

## June 2–3 — Microsoft Build 2026

**Where:** Fort Mason, San Francisco
**Keynote:** June 2, streaming free at [build.microsoft.com](https://build.microsoft.com)
**Expected attendees:** [~2,500 in-person developers](https://www.alltechnerd.com/microsoft-build-2026-set-for-san-francisco/) — down from past editions' roughly 5,000, per pre-event reporting

Satya Nadella's developer conference this year is smaller and more focused than past editions — but the concentration on AI agents makes it one of the more important events of the year for developers building on Azure.

**What's expected:**

- **Multi-agent orchestration APIs.** [Build 2025 introduced the autonomous GitHub Copilot coding agent](https://sdtimes.com/ai-agents/microsoft-build-github-copilot-coding-agent-azure-ai-foundry-updates-support-for-mcp-and-more/), which opens pull requests that require human approval before running. Build 2026 is expected to formalize the runtime primitives that make agent-to-agent communication production-ready.
- **Azure AI Foundry update.** [The catalog crossed 1,900 models at Build 2025](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/blog/all-the-azure-news-you-dont-want-to-miss-from-microsoft-build-2025/). The 2026 focus is expected to be deeper enterprise integrations — combined routing across OpenAI models, open-source alternatives, and SLMs optimized for Windows on-device inference.
- **GitHub Copilot next generation.** Real-world deployment learnings from the autonomous coding agent since Build 2025, plus announced next-generation capabilities.
- **Windows AI integration.** Copilot Runtime updates, Windows on Arm progress, and new security APIs that lock down the desktop.

**Update (checked 2026-07-29):** Nadella's actual June 2 keynote made [Agent Mode the default across Word, Excel, and PowerPoint in Office 365 Copilot](https://www.technobezz.com/news/microsoft-opens-build-2026-in-san-francisco-with-agent-mode-as-the-default-for-office-365-copilot), framing agents as "async coworkers that can execute long-running tasks," and [Microsoft used the event to make agent governance and identity controls a headline security story](https://securitypointbreak.com/2026/06/02/microsofts-not-so-quiet-security-pitch-at-build-2026/) — largely matching the multi-agent and Windows-integration expectations above.

## June 8–12 — WWDC 2026

**Where:** Apple Park, Cupertino
**Keynote:** June 8, 10:00 a.m. PT
**Streaming:** [apple.com/apple-events](https://www.apple.com/apple-events/) (free)

This is [Tim Cook's final WWDC as CEO](https://www.republicworld.com/tech/apple-wwdc-2026-set-for-june-8-ios-27-macos-27-a-siri-overhaul-and-tim-cook-s-final-keynote-as-ceo-2026-05-19-124930) — which would make it a significant transition moment even without a packed AI agenda. Add the Siri overhaul that Apple has been quietly building for two years, and this becomes one of the most consequential AI announcements of the year.

**What's confirmed and expected:**

- **Siri 2.0 ("Campos").** A ground-up redesign of Siri, shifting from a voice assistant to something closer to a chatbot interface. Conversation history. Context across apps. A standalone Siri app. Dynamic Island integration when active.
- **iOS 27 and macOS 27.** Both expected to follow Apple's "Snow Leopard" philosophy this cycle — stability and performance over new features, with AI threaded through rather than bolted on.
- **Google Gemini as AI backbone.** Apple's roughly [$1 billion-a-year deal with Google](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/12/apple-google-ai-siri-gemini.html), [reported by Bloomberg in November 2025](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-05/apple-plans-to-use-1-2-trillion-parameter-google-gemini-model-to-power-new-siri) to cover a custom 1.2-trillion-parameter Gemini model, brings that model into Apple Intelligence. Expect the first on-device integration to debut here.
- **Third-party AI model system.** Reports suggest Apple will allow users to route AI requests to third-party models — OpenAI, Google, potentially Anthropic — through an extension-style interface integrated with Writing Tools and Image Playground.

**Update (checked 2026-07-29):** Apple's actual June 8 keynote confirmed the Siri overhaul, iOS 27/macOS 27, and the Gemini partnership as expected. It also went further than the "reports suggest" framing above: [Apple introduced iOS 27 Extensions](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/06/09/apple-outlines-major-ai-and-developer-tool-updates/), letting users set Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, or Grok as their preferred AI provider across Siri, Writing Tools, and Image Playground via a dedicated marketplace — [confirmed as a formal third-party AI framework, not just a rumor](https://www.itbrew.com/stories/apple-opens-the-door-to-third-party-ai-model-vendors-in-latest-update).

## June 11–12 — SpaceX IPO ($SPCX)

**Exchange:** Nasdaq
**Ticker:** SPCX
**Pricing:** June 11 (after market close)
**Trading debut:** June 12

The largest IPO in history — by expected market cap — prices in under two weeks. [SpaceX's up-to-30% retail allocation is roughly triple the typical retail share](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/21/retail-investors-get-direct-access-to-spacex-ipo-through-major-brokerage-platforms.html). [Robinhood, Fidelity, Schwab, SoFi, and E\*TRADE are the platforms with confirmed retail access](https://fortune.com/2026/06/08/retail-investors-participate-spacex-ipo-spcx-elon-musk-brokerages/).

The week of June 8 is the roadshow. Pricing follows on June 11 after close. June 12 is first day of trading.

Retail investors should set expectations clearly: demand will far exceed supply, partial fills and zero allocation are the most common outcome for most applicants, and the open market on June 12 is likely the realistic entry point for anyone who didn't get allocation.

**Update (checked 2026-07-29):** [the IPO priced at $135/share](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/09/spacex-ipo-explained-stock-price-date.html) and began trading June 12 as expected, opening around $150 and closing its first day near $161 — a roughly 19% first-day gain. Each broker set its own anti-flipping rule for retail allottees: Fidelity's window was 15 calendar days, Robinhood's was 30 days (with a 60-day IPO-access ban for violators), and SoFi could charge a $50 fee for sales within 120 days.

## Watch List: Unscheduled, Expected Before July

Three major model releases have no confirmed date, but are widely expected before the end of June:

### Grok 5 — xAI

[xAI originally targeted Q1 2026, which passed without a release; xAI's official account then pointed to Q2 2026 as the revised target](https://www.nxcode.io/resources/news/grok-5-release-date-latest-news-2026). [Polymarket puts the probability of a public Grok 5 release before June 30 at approximately 33% as of late May](https://polymarket.com/event/grok-5-released-by).

Grok 5 is reported to be training on [xAI's Colossus 2 supercluster in Memphis](https://www.zbuild.io/resources/news/grok-5-release-date-6t-parameters-agi-xai-complete-guide-2026) — not the original Colossus facility. Reported parameter counts vary by source: most coverage puts Grok 5 itself at roughly 6 trillion parameters (Colossus 2 is reportedly also training separate runs up to 10 trillion parameters), so the 10-trillion figure may describe a different training run rather than the Grok 5 model that ships.

**Update (checked 2026-07-29):** the Q2 window closed without a release. As of July, Grok 5 [remains in training with availability slipping to Q3 2026 or later](https://www.nxcode.io/resources/news/grok-5-release-date-latest-news-2026), and Polymarket's "by June 30" contract resolved No.

**Update (checked 2026-08-24):** still no Grok 5 release, and no firmer date from xAI. What xAI actually shipped in the interim was a smaller model — the roughly 1.5-trillion-parameter "V9" model launched as **Grok 4.5** on July 8, 2026 — while [Grok 5 itself remains in training on the Colossus 2 supercluster](https://www.nxcode.io/resources/news/grok-5-release-date-latest-news-2026), with estimates still clustering around Q3 2026 or later. No public model card, spec sheet, or company-issued release date exists as of this update.

### GPT-5.6 — OpenAI

[Polymarket puts GPT-5.6's release before June 30 at approximately 80-89% as of late May/early June](https://polymarket.com/event/gpt-5pt6-released-by) — the highest-confidence model release prediction on the calendar. No official announcement from OpenAI as of this writing.

**Update (checked 2026-07-29):** OpenAI announced the GPT-5.6 series on June 27, but it stayed in limited preview — reportedly restricted to a small number of government-approved organizations — rather than shipping to the public. Polymarket's June 30 contract ultimately resolved No.

**Update (checked 2026-08-24):** GPT-5.6 has since gone fully public. [OpenAI declared the GPT-5.6 family (Sol, Terra, and Luna) generally available across ChatGPT, Codex, and the API on July 9, 2026](https://www.engadget.com/2210308/openai-rolls-out-gpt5-6-july-9/), after the U.S. Department of Commerce's Center for AI Standards and Innovation completed additional review and granted expedited approval to move beyond the restricted government-partner preview. [CNBC covered the same expansion](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/08/openai-expanding-gpt-5point6-ai-model-release-ending-government-limits.html). As of this update, GPT-5.6 remains generally available with no further access restrictions reported.

### Gemini 3.5 Pro — Google

[Sundar Pichai announced Gemini 3.5 Flash at Google I/O 2026 (May 19) and said Gemini 3.5 Pro would follow in June](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/sundar-pichai-io-2026/) — a line that reportedly drew audible groans from the live audience, since Pro was the version developers were most anticipating.

**Update (checked 2026-07-29):** Pro's general availability slipped well past June. [Bloomberg reported in mid-July that Google had fallen behind schedule while working to improve the model's coding performance](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320308/20260713/gemini-35-pro-targets-july-17-after-full-rebuild-every-spec-remains-unconfirmed.htm), and as of late July it remained in limited partner testing rather than broadly available.

**Update (checked 2026-08-24):** still not GA. [Bloomberg reported July 16 that the July 17 target had also slipped](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/16/alphabet-stock-gemini-3-5-pro-ai.html), with coding-performance shortfalls again cited as the cause. A month later, [Bloomberg reported on August 13 that Gemini 3.5 Pro was still delayed](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/google-debuts-new-gemini-flash-while-top-ai-model-still-delayed) — now having missed three separate targets (June, mid-July, and early August) — and was notably absent from Google's own August 13 Gemini 3.7 Flash launch. As of this update it remains in limited Vertex AI enterprise partner testing, not generally available.

## Also Watching: Anthropic/Pentagon DC Circuit Ruling

On [May 19, a three-judge panel heard oral arguments](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/19/anthropic-dod-blacklist-court-opening-arguments.html) in Anthropic's appeal of the Department of Defense's supply chain risk designation, a label the company has argued has effectively barred it from federal contracting since Defense Secretary Hegseth's designation earlier in 2026.

The panel appeared divided. [Judge Karen Henderson said the designation was "a spectacular overreach" and that she saw no evidence supporting a supply-chain-risk finding](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/05/appeals-court-judges-appear-to-be-divided-over-pentagons-legal-dispute-with-ai-company-anthropic/), while Judges Katsas and Rao appeared more sympathetic to the government's position. Separately, [a federal judge in San Francisco, Rita Lin, had already granted Anthropic a preliminary injunction in March blocking enforcement of the designation](https://www.npr.org/2026/03/26/nx-s1-5762971/judge-temporarily-blocks-anthropic-ban), calling it "likely both contrary to law and arbitrary and capricious" — though [the DC Circuit itself had separately denied Anthropic's request for an emergency stay in April](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/08/anthropic-pentagon-court-ruling-supply-chain-risk.html), so the designation has continued to affect DOD contracting even with the SF injunction in place.

A DC Circuit ruling — which could come any time — would either reinstate that block or allow the Pentagon's designation to stand pending full appellate review. As of this audit (2026-07-29), no written opinion has been found in current reporting; the panel's decision remains pending more than two months after oral argument. Given the scale of Anthropic's federal contracts and the precedent for other AI companies, it is one of the more consequential pending legal decisions in the industry.

**Update (checked 2026-08-24):** still no written merits opinion found in current reporting. The only DC Circuit action on record remains the April 8 denial of Anthropic's emergency stay request pending appeal — a procedural ruling on interim relief, not a decision on the merits of the underlying appeal argued May 19. More than three months after oral argument, the panel's merits ruling remains pending.
