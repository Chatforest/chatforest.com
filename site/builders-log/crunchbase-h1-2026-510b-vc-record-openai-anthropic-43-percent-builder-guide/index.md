# Crunchbase H1 2026: $510B VC Record — and Why 43% Went to Two AI Labs

> Global venture funding hit $510B in the first half of 2026 — more than all of 2025. OpenAI and Anthropic alone captured $217B (43% of the total). Here's what that concentration means for builders depending on this infrastructure.


**We research these topics — we do not have direct access to private financial data or insider sources.** All figures come from Crunchbase's published H1 2026 report, companies' own announcements (Anthropic, and Elon Musk's public statements on X), and reporting by Bloomberg, CNBC, Forbes, Fortune, NBC News, and AI Weekly. Inline links throughout point to the specific source for each figure.

---

Crunchbase published its [H1 2026 global startup funding report](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/global-startup-exits-ipo-ma-soar-ai-q2-h1-2026/) on July 2, and the headline number is hard to internalize: **$510 billion** invested in the first six months of 2026 alone.

That exceeds the [previous record for any half-year period ($375B in H2 2021) by 36%](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/global-startup-exits-ipo-ma-soar-ai-q2-h1-2026/). It exceeds [all of 2025 ($440B)](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/global-startup-exits-ipo-ma-soar-ai-q2-h1-2026/) by 16%. It happened in six months.

But the headline isn't the story. The story is where the money went — and what happens to the builders sitting downstream.

## The concentration that matters for builders

Of the $510B, **[OpenAI and Anthropic captured $217 billion](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/global-startup-exits-ipo-ma-soar-ai-q2-h1-2026/)** — [43% of all global startup funding in the first half of the year](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/openai-and-anthropic-take-43-of-h1-2026-venture-funding).

That's one investment category (frontier AI model companies) absorbing nearly half of all global venture capital. Not "AI broadly," not "tech" — two specific labs.

The breakdown: Anthropic [raised $65B in a Series H round that closed in late May](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h), reaching a [$965B valuation that made it the most valuable private company in the US](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/anthropic-secures-965-billion-valuation-raising-65-billion-rcna347390), surpassing OpenAI's prior disclosed valuation. OpenAI's own financing accounts for most of the remainder: it [closed a $122B round in March at an $852B valuation](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-31/openai-valued-at-852-billion-after-completing-122-billion-round), backstopped in part by [SoftBank's $40B bridge loan facility used to fund its share of that round](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-27/softbank-secures-record-40-billion-bridge-loan-for-openai-stake).

**What this concentration means in practice:**

1. **These labs are not going anywhere.** At this funding level, both OpenAI and Anthropic have enough capital runway to sustain frontier model development through the decade regardless of near-term revenue. The common developer fear — "what if my primary API provider shuts down?" — is not the risk it was in 2023.

2. **Pricing pressure will continue.** OpenAI and Anthropic are both using capital to subsidize aggressive pricing. Neither is profitable at current model costs; both are growing revenue rapidly enough that investors are comfortable with the burn. For builders, this means the token-price wars continue — with both labs motivated to win developer mindshare before their IPOs.

3. **Everything else has to fight for the remaining 57%.** Every other AI company — inference providers, tooling startups, vertical AI applications — competed for $293B. That's still a historically large market, but it's the context in which you evaluate funding announcements from smaller players.

## AI's share of Q2 capital

For the first time, [more than **70% of global startup capital in a quarter went to AI-focused companies**](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/global-startup-exits-ipo-ma-soar-ai-q2-h1-2026/) — up from just under 50% a year earlier, per Crunchbase.

This is the venture market repricing toward AI conviction. If you raise a non-AI startup today, you are competing against the most lopsided capital allocation to a single sector in venture history.

For builders, this means:
- The tooling ecosystem around AI (observability, evaluation, deployment, security) is well-funded. Expect more tools, more competition, more consolidation.
- AI-adjacent verticals (legal AI, health AI, finance AI) will be overfunded in some areas, creating both opportunity and noise.
- Infrastructure assumptions — GPU access, inference throughput, hosting cost — are all moving faster than non-AI historical norms because the capital is there to accelerate them.

## The mega-events that shaped the numbers

**SpaceX IPO ($75B raise, $1.77T valuation):** [SpaceX's Nasdaq debut](https://finance.yahoo.com/video/spacex-raises-75b-in-historic-ipo-valued-at-177-trillion-133425218.html) [raised $75B at a $1.77T valuation](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/11/spacex-raises-75-billion-in-record-setting-ipo-ahead-of-nasdaq-debut.html), among the largest IPOs of any kind on record. SpaceX going public changed the exit math for every late-stage investor and created liquidity that partly recycled into AI deals.

**SpaceX acquires Anysphere / Cursor ($60B):** [SpaceX agreed to acquire Cursor's parent company Anysphere for $60B in an all-stock deal](https://www.forbes.com/sites/sandycarter/2026/06/16/spacex-buys-cursor-in-largest-startup-acquisition-ever-at-60-billion/) — [the largest startup acquisition on record](https://devops.com/spacex-to-acquire-ai-coding-leader-cursor-in-60-billion-blockbuster-deal/), disclosed less than a week after SpaceX's own IPO. This matters to builders directly: [Elon Musk confirmed that Grok 4.5, built on xAI's 1.5T V9 foundation model, added Cursor data in supplemental training](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2071184354756477041) ahead of its private beta at SpaceX and Tesla ([independently reported](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260630-grok-4-5-cursor/)). Neither Musk's post nor subsequent reporting specifies whether that data was anonymized. The coding tool you use may now be feeding the model your work is evaluated against.

**Anthropic Q2 raise ($65B):** [This closed Anthropic as the most valuable private company in the US](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/anthropic-secures-965-billion-valuation-raising-65-billion-rcna347390) — ahead of OpenAI on that metric as of mid-year. The round [included a previously-committed $5B investment from Amazon](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h), and [both Amazon and Google have billions riding on Anthropic's outcome](https://fortune.com/2026/06/04/amazon-google-billions-anthropic-ipo/) through prior compute and investment commitments — a dependency that shapes API availability, regional compliance, and model roadmap decisions.

## Q2 exits: IPOs and acquisitions at record pace

Exit activity matched the funding pace:
- **[32 companies went public](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/global-startup-exits-ipo-ma-soar-ai-q2-h1-2026/)** at valuations above $1 billion in Q2 — [one of the strongest venture-backed IPO quarters since the 2021 boom](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/5-charts-vc-backed-ipos-225013160.html)
- **[24 acquisitions](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/global-startup-exits-ipo-ma-soar-ai-q2-h1-2026/)** at prices at or above $1 billion, totaling $113B — the highest quarter on record for venture-backed M&A

Anthropic has already taken a concrete step toward going public: [it confidentially filed for an IPO](https://fortune.com/2026/06/04/amazon-google-billions-anthropic-ipo/) around the time of its Series H round. OpenAI has not yet filed, though it closed its own [$122B round in March](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-31/openai-valued-at-852-billion-after-completing-122-billion-round). When either lab goes public, its reporting obligations shift dramatically. Builders will have access to quarterly disclosures on API revenue, cost structure, and model economics that don't exist today. That will clarify the real cost of the models you're using and whether current pricing is sustainable.

## Geographic distribution

[The U.S. dominated Q2 funding with roughly two-thirds of global capital — down from 83% in Q1](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/global-startup-exits-ipo-ma-soar-ai-q2-h1-2026/), as European and Asian AI deals picked up. Part of the Q1 concentration reflects [OpenAI closing its $122B round in late March](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-31/openai-valued-at-852-billion-after-completing-122-billion-round), a U.S. deal large enough on its own to skew the quarter's geography; Anthropic's raise, which closed in [late May](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h), falls in Q2 and is part of what made that quarter more distributed.

For builders outside the US, this shift matters: non-US venture interest in AI tooling, compliance layers, and regional model deployment is growing. If you're building for GDPR jurisdictions or Asia-Pacific markets, the funding landscape for infrastructure you'd depend on is improving.

## The builder calculus

The H1 2026 numbers point in a consistent direction:

**Stay on the frontier labs for core model capability.** With $217B absorbed by OpenAI and Anthropic alone, neither is at risk of competitive collapse in any near-term scenario. Both are motivated by upcoming IPOs to expand developer adoption. Both are in an active token-price war. This is a reasonable time to build on their APIs.

**Diversify at the infrastructure layer.** The Cursor acquisition is a warning: the tools you rely on get acquired, and acquisitions change training data relationships and product direction. Build where you can swap the underlying inference provider without rewriting your integration.

**Watch the exit market.** OpenAI and Anthropic going public will be the most significant AI-industry information event since GPT-4. The S-1 disclosures will reveal real cost structures, real margin profiles, and real API economics. Build your roadmap with that information event in mind — decisions made before you see the filings may look different after.
