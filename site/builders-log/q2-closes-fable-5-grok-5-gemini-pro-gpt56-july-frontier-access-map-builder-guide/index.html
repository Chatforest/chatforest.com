Fable 5 (Anthropic) — Day 18, July 8 Is the Next Gate

Fable 5 is the most consequential and most covered delay on this list. It was in market for three days (June 9–12), then suspended under a US Department of Commerce export control directive. Anthropic’s own statement says the government’s letter “did not provide specific details of its national security concern” but that Anthropic’s understanding was the government had “become aware of a method of bypassing, or ‘jailbreaking’ Fable 5” — using it to identify software vulnerabilities in a codebase. Anthropic first disclosed the shutdown on X, and CNBC covered the June 12 directive the same day. That was eighteen days ago.

What changed since the June 19 update:

On June 26, the US government notified Anthropic that Mythos 5 — the cybersecurity-oriented model in the same class — can be redeployed to more than 100 pre-approved critical infrastructure organizations in the US, per TechCrunch’s reporting and NBC News on Lutnick’s letter. That partial restoration signals that the review framework is working through the model family, not stonewalling it.

For Fable 5 (the general-access version), the Pentagon and NSA still have not signed off as of this writing. Prediction-market odds of restoration by July 1 were volatile through late June — Polymarket’s market swung from roughly 73% in mid-June down to the mid-to-high 50s in the final days, while Kalshi traders put it near 58% — those markets are on track to resolve NO today. The next structural anchor is July 8, when Anthropic’s updated privacy policy takes effect, introducing government-issued ID verification and biometric data (via third-party vendor Persona). Analysts see this as a potential US-citizens-only restoration path — a technical mechanism that could satisfy export control requirements by verifying that users are US persons.

The August 1 deadline matters too: that is the 60-day mark on Trump’s June 2 executive order directing federal agencies to build a framework for early government access to frontier models. The export control action against Fable 5 was downstream of that order. If the Pentagon-NSA review completes inside the 60-day window, August 1 becomes a hard backstop.

Builder action:

  • For builders currently planning on Fable 5: Claude Opus 4.8 (released May 28, no dedicated Anthropic blog post; Opus 4.8 has since been superseded by Opus 5) and Haiku 4.5 are available in the API today via Claude.ai, Amazon Bedrock, and Google Vertex AI. If your use case is agentic coding, Claude Code with Sonnet 4.6 is in full production use.
  • Watch July 8 specifically: if Anthropic rolls out Persona verification targeting US users, a restoration announcement could follow within days.
  • Do not block product decisions on a July date without a named date. The July 8 path is plausible but not confirmed.

Grok 5 (xAI) — Second Consecutive Quarterly Miss

Grok 5 has now missed two consecutive quarterly deadlines. xAI raised $20 billion in a January 2026 Series E round that pushed its valuation to $230 billion; around that time Elon Musk targeted Q1 2026 for Grok 5, then the window slipped to Q2. Q2 ends today. No official Grok 5 announcement, no release date, and no API documentation exists for the model.

What this model is supposed to be: A 6-trillion-parameter flagship using a mixture-of-experts architecture, trained to match or exceed frontier labs on reasoning, coding, and scientific tasks, with native video understanding. xAI has framed it as their AGI-track model. The training compute required is substantial; xAI’s Colossus 2 cluster in Memphis is reportedly still running training runs.

What’s available now: xAI has been actively releasing mid-tier models this quarter rather than the flagship:

  • Grok 4.3: Available in API. 1M token context, native video input, native PDF/PPTX/XLSX file generation, priced at $1.25/$2.50 per million input/output tokens. The current best xAI model for production use.
  • Grok Build 0.1: Public beta via API, priced at $1/million input and $2/million output tokens. Smaller, purpose-built for coding agents. Available now.
  • Grok 4.5: Private beta, a 1.5-trillion-parameter “V9” model. Accessed via SpaceX and Tesla internal deployments only; Musk claims performance “close to, perhaps exceeding” Claude Opus, but xAI has not submitted it to any public benchmark or given outside parties access. No general API access.

Builder action: Q3 2026 (July–September) is the realistic window for Grok 5 API access, but xAI has given no specific date. Do not hold architecture decisions for Grok 5. Evaluate Grok 4.3 as the current xAI ceiling for production use.

Gemini 3.5 Pro (Google) — June Window Closed, July Confirmed

Gemini 3.5 Pro’s missed June window is the most predictable miss on this list. At Google I/O on May 19, Sundar Pichai said: “We’re also excited for Gemini 3.5 Pro. We are using it internally, it’s showing great improvements, and it will be coming next month.” That was a June commitment. Business Insider reported on June 27 that the target has slipped to July, with Google using the extra time to “collect input from early testers” and apply “learnings from its Flash model” — Google declined to comment officially on the record.

What happened: Gemini 3.5 Pro prioritizes long-context coding tasks and “Deep Think” reasoning at scale, which the Business Insider report says needed more refinement time than Flash’s profile required. Separately, Gemini co-lead Noam Shazeer left for OpenAI (announced June 18) and AlphaFold lead John Jumper left DeepMind for Anthropic (announced June 19) — about a week before the delay was reported. The Business Insider delay report does not link the departures to the slip, and the timeline suggests the departures predate whatever caused the delay rather than causing it.

What’s available now: Gemini 3.5 Flash is in full GA — the default model in the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search. API pricing is $1.50/$9.00 per million input/output tokens.

Builder action: For mid-tier, high-speed tasks, Flash is strong and fully available. For use cases requiring the 2M context window or Deep Think ($250/month Ultra gate), plan for mid-to-late July. The Vertex AI enterprise preview remains the only current access path for Pro.

GPT-5.6 Sol/Terra/Luna (OpenAI) — Launched, But Not For You

GPT-5.6 is technically not a miss — OpenAI launched it on June 26, on schedule with the June 22–28 prediction market window. The issue is access. All three tiers (Sol, Terra, Luna) are currently restricted to approximately 20 government-vetted organizations, each individually approved, after the White House’s Office of the National Cyber Director and Office of Science and Technology Policy asked OpenAI — on a nominally voluntary basis, citing Sol’s cybersecurity capabilities — to limit the June 26 launch. This traces back to Trump’s June 2 executive order, but the order itself sets up a voluntary framework, not a mandatory review requirement.

What launched:

  • Sol: Flagship tier. Terminal-Bench 2.1 score of 91.9% in ultra mode (new SOTA, 3.9 points ahead of the next-best model). Multi-subagent ultra reasoning.
  • Terra: Balanced everyday model. The likely default once GA.
  • Luna: Fast, high-volume tier. The cost-optimized option.

Pricing: $5/$30 per million input/output tokens — same as GPT-5.5’s rate card.

What’s available now: GPT-5.5 and GPT-5.5 Pro are in full API access (GA in Microsoft Foundry since April 24). For most production workloads, GPT-5.5 Pro covers the use case gap until GPT-5.6 Terra or Luna achieve GA.

Builder action: The end-of-July GA estimate for GPT-5.6 is market consensus, not an OpenAI commitment. If your roadmap needs GPT-5.6 Sol’s reasoning capability specifically, plan for Q3 2026 as your evaluation window. For everything else, GPT-5.5 Pro is the reasonable production bet.

What July Is Actually About

The four-model delay story has concentrated attention on what’s missing. But the past month has also surfaced what is arriving in July:

  • July 6: OpenAI Workspace Agents move to credit-based pricing after a free preview period — persistent agents that connect to external apps and run multi-step workflows in ChatGPT Business/Enterprise/Edu. This ships next week.
  • July 8: Anthropic Persona ID verification takes effect — the potential Fable 5 trigger (see above).
  • July 10: SK Hynix’s Nasdaq ADR debut — a planned $29B listing, per SK Hynix’s own newsroom.
  • Mid-to-late July: Gemini 3.5 Pro GA target.
  • End of July: GPT-5.6 general access target (market consensus at time of writing — see editor’s note above on the actual July 9 GA date).
  • August 1: 60-day government review deadline under Trump’s executive order.

July is not a waiting month — it’s the delivery month. Builders who have been evaluating against current models are well-positioned to move fast when access opens.
