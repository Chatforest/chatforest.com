**Summary:** In April 2026, [Anthropic's annualized revenue crossed $30 billion](https://venturebeat.com/technology/anthropic-says-it-hit-a-30-billion-revenue-run-rate-after-crazy-80x-growth), passing OpenAI's reported $24–25 billion for the first time. The number matters, but the growth rate — $1B to $30B in 15 months — is the structural story. Claude Code drove a significant share of the acceleration. OpenAI disputes $8 billion of the figure on accounting grounds. A new Anthropic funding round targeting a $900 billion valuation is reportedly closing the week of May 26. Part of our **[AI Industry Analysis](/categories/ai-tools/)** coverage.

---

## The Growth Trajectory

Here is Anthropic's ARR progression since early 2024:

| Date | ARR |
|------|-----|
| January 2024 | ~$87M |
| December 2024 | ~$1B |
| End of 2025 | ~$9B |
| February 2026 | ~$14B |
| March 2026 | ~$19B |
| April 2026 | ~$30B |
| May 2026 | ~$44B (SemiAnalysis) |

That is roughly 30x in 15 months from January 2025 to April 2026 — with [$21 billion of that added in the four months between end-of-2025 and April 2026 alone](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-06/broadcom-confirms-deal-to-ship-google-tpu-chips-to-anthropic). For context: [AWS took 13 years to reach $35B in annual revenue](https://fourweekmba.com/aws-revenues/) (launched 2006, crossed $35B in 2019), and [Salesforce took over 20 years to pass $20B](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/crm/revenue/) (founded 1999, crossed $20B in fiscal year 2021).

Over the same period, [OpenAI's trajectory was comparatively modest: from roughly $20–21B ARR at end of 2025 to approximately $25B by February 2026](https://mlq.ai/news/openai-achieves-over-20-billion-annual-revenue-run-rate-amid-explosive-growth/) — about 20–25% growth. That's still substantial by any normal standard. Relative to Anthropic's pace, it represents a structural shift in who is accumulating enterprise AI revenue.

## Why Claude Code Changed the Math

The single most important factor in Anthropic's acceleration is Claude Code, its AI-assisted coding agent launched publicly in May 2025.

[Claude Code became generally available in May 2025, hit $1 billion in annualized revenue by November 2025 — about six months after launch — and reached $2.5 billion in annualized revenue by February 2026](https://sacra.com/c/anthropic/), more than doubling from where it had started the year.

The downstream effect was material: [the number of companies spending $1 million or more annually with Anthropic more than doubled — from roughly 500 to over 1,000 — in the two months before April 2026](https://sacra.com/c/anthropic/), a figure [Anthropic itself confirmed alongside the $30 billion run-rate announcement](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-06/broadcom-confirms-deal-to-ship-google-tpu-chips-to-anthropic). That kind of customer-level spend compression is typically a sign of developer-driven adoption spreading upward into budget.

## The Enterprise vs. Consumer Split

This is where Anthropic's structural position diverges most clearly from OpenAI's.

Approximately [80% of Anthropic's revenue comes from enterprise API usage and developer contracts, generated by its 300,000+ business customers](https://sacra.com/c/anthropic/). [OpenAI's composition runs roughly 60% consumer subscriptions (ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Team) and 40% enterprise, with enterprise on track for parity by end of 2026](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/openai-vs-anthropic-statistics/). This matters for several reasons:

**Revenue durability.** Enterprise contracts are stickier than consumer subscriptions. They involve procurement processes, multi-year commitments, and integration work that creates switching costs. Consumer subscribers cancel during product slowdowns.

**Unit economics.** Enterprise API revenue typically carries higher margins at scale because the workloads are predictable and the customers do their own cost optimization. Consumer subscription revenue requires ongoing product investment and churn management.

**Valuation multiples.** Enterprise SaaS businesses trade at higher multiples than consumer subscription businesses — a distinction that will matter significantly if either company pursues an IPO in late 2026 or 2027.

## The Efficiency Gap

Anthropic's revenue story would be less significant if it were being purchased entirely by cost. The margin data suggests it isn't.

[Anthropic's blended gross margin rose from roughly -94% in 2024 to the mid-60% range by Q2 2026, with the core API business margin exceeding 80%, according to SemiAnalysis estimates](https://finance.biggo.com/news/02d45650-b569-4d12-b44d-8d6d8aeb6a0d). That is a substantial improvement — driven by model efficiency gains (each generation of Claude requiring less compute per token to run), hardware scaling on AWS's infrastructure, and the compounding effect of workload predictability at enterprise scale.

On training costs, projections diverge sharply: [confidential financial documents reviewed by the Wall Street Journal show OpenAI projecting roughly $121 billion in compute/training spending by 2028, while Anthropic's comparable projection peaks around $30 billion in the same period](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/openai-anthropic-ipo-finances-04b3cfb9) — roughly one-quarter the cost. If those projections hold, Anthropic would be sustaining frontier-model capabilities at a structurally lower cost base. The reasons are debated (architecture differences, efficiency research, different risk tolerance on frontier scale), but the gap is large enough to be worth flagging.

## The Accounting Dispute

[OpenAI's Chief Revenue Officer Denise Dresser sent a four-page internal memo to staff arguing that Anthropic's $30 billion figure overstates actual revenue by approximately $8 billion](https://www.implicator.ai/openai-cro-tells-staff-anthropic-inflates-run-rate-by-8-billion/), a memo [independently reported by Gizmodo after being viewed by CNBC and The Verge](https://gizmodo.com/openai-exec-reveals-new-strategy-in-leaked-memo-attack-anthropic-2000745872).

The dispute is methodological, not fraudulent. Anthropic reports on a **gross basis**: it books the full amount a customer pays for Claude services through platforms like AWS and Google Cloud, including the portion paid as commission to the cloud partner. OpenAI, in its Microsoft partnership, uses a **net basis**, recognizing only the revenue it retains after Microsoft's share is deducted. Both methods are permissible under GAAP. Dresser's own framing, per the memo: "They use accounting treatment that makes revenue look bigger than it is, including grossing up rev share with Amazon and Google."

Under OpenAI's preferred calculation, Anthropic's comparable figure would be approximately $22 billion — which would place it below OpenAI's $24–25 billion.

The practical effect of the dispute is narrow: it does not change the growth rate, the enterprise composition, or the efficiency metrics. What it does change is the specific crossing point — whether Anthropic passed OpenAI in April 2026 or whether it will pass on a net basis later. The memo is also a signal that OpenAI is paying close attention and chose to respond internally rather than publicly — a competitive posture, not an accounting clarification.

## The Funding Context

[In February 2026, Anthropic closed a $30 billion Series G round led by GIC and Coatue, co-led by D. E. Shaw Ventures, Dragoneer, Founders Fund, ICONIQ, and MGX, at a $380 billion post-money valuation](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-raises-30-billion-series-g-funding-380-billion-post-money-valuation) — coming less than a year after OpenAI's own $40 billion round in March 2025 at a $300 billion valuation, which was the largest private tech fundraise up to that point.

As of late May 2026, [Anthropic is reportedly closing an additional round at a pre-money valuation above $900 billion, expected to close as soon as the week of May 26](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-12/anthropic-in-talks-to-raise-30-billion-at-900-billion-valuation). If that valuation holds, [Anthropic would surpass OpenAI's reported $852 billion post-money valuation from March 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jonmarkman/2026/05/04/anthropics-900b-funding-round-set-to-surpass-openai/) as the most valuable private AI startup in the world.

The implications of two AI companies with combined valuations approaching $2 trillion are not trivial — particularly given that most enterprise software comparables trade at substantially lower multiples on actual revenue. Both companies are pricing in trajectory, not current state. The revenue data, especially Anthropic's, at least gives trajectory something to anchor to.

---

## What This Means

The revenue crossing in April 2026 is significant. But the more durable observation is structural:

1. **Claude Code is an enterprise-grade revenue driver, not a developer tool.** The speed at which it crossed $1B ARR and pulled enterprise accounts with it suggests agentic coding tools are buying-center-level products, not department-level subscriptions.

2. **Anthropic's enterprise mix is more durable than OpenAI's consumer mix** if consumer subscription growth slows. Whether it slows is an open question — but the composition difference is a real risk factor for OpenAI.

3. **The accounting dispute is a proxy battle for IPO narrative.** Both companies are managing investor perceptions ahead of potential 2026–2027 public offerings. Dresser's memo was about more than $8 billion; it was about which company controls the comparative framing going into the roadshow.

4. **The efficiency gap matters more over time.** A company running at $30B in projected training cost by 2028 against a competitor projecting $121B faces a different structural ceiling. Whether those projections hold is uncertain. The direction is not.

The number itself — $30B ARR — is large. The 30x growth in 15 months is historically anomalous. The question that follows, as with every prior AI revenue milestone, is whether the trajectory is durable or whether it reflects a compression of early enterprise adoption that eventually mean-reverts. Claude Code's $2.5B run rate within nine months of launch, and the 1,000-company $1M+ cohort, suggest the enterprise adoption is more than early noise.
