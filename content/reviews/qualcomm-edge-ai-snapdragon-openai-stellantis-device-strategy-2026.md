On May 22, 2026, [Qualcomm shares jumped 12% in a single session, hitting a record high](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/22/qualcomms-stock-pop-shows-investors-waking-up-to-boom-in-ai-devices.html) after [news broke that Stellantis had expanded its Snapdragon partnership](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/21/3299417/0/en/stellantis-and-qualcomm-expand-partnership-to-adopt-snapdragon-digital-chassis-driver-assistance-cockpit-and-connectivity-platforms-across-next-generation-vehicle-architectures.html) across its entire vehicle lineup. But the single-day pop was a footnote. The real story: [Qualcomm stock had already surged roughly 75% over the prior month](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/22/qualcomms-stock-pop-shows-investors-waking-up-to-boom-in-ai-devices.html) as investors finally started connecting the dots on what the company actually is — the unchosen, invisible backbone of nearly every AI device anyone's building.

## What Triggered the Pop

The immediate catalyst was a [multi-year deal with Stellantis, announced May 21](https://www.qualcomm.com/news/releases/2026/05/stellantis-and-qualcomm-expand-partnership-to-adopt----snapdrago). Under the expanded agreement:

- **Snapdragon Digital Chassis** SoCs will power cockpit, connectivity, and ADAS across Stellantis' global vehicle portfolio
- **Snapdragon Ride Pilot** — Qualcomm's Level 2+ hands-free automated driving platform — is included
- A non-binding letter of intent was signed for **aiMotive**, Stellantis' automated driving and simulation subsidiary, to join Qualcomm Technologies

That acquisition, if completed, would bring [a company Stellantis itself calls "a leading artificial intelligence and autonomous driving start-up"](https://www.stellantis.com/en/news/press-releases/2022/november/stellantis-accelerates-autonomous-driving-journey-with-acquisition-of-aimotive-a-leading-artificial-intelligence-and-autonomous-driving-start-up) — [aiMotive is based in Budapest, Hungary](https://aimotive.com/) — directly inside Qualcomm, strengthening its push into full-stack autonomous driving rather than just chipsets.

The deal pushed [Qualcomm's automotive segment past $5 billion in annualized revenue — a record](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/8831042/qualcomm-inc-qcom-q2-2026-earnings-call-highlights-record-automotive-revenues-and-strategic-ai-engagements), and reinforced a pattern investors hadn't fully priced in: Qualcomm's auto business isn't a hedge. It's becoming the point.

## The Earnings Were Already Strong

Before the Stellantis news, [Qualcomm had already posted a clean Q2 FY2026 beat on April 29](https://www.tikr.com/blog/qualcomm-reports-10-6b-in-q2-revenue-with-eps-at-high-end-of-guidance). Revenue came in at **$10.6 billion**, EPS at **$2.65**, both above consensus. The segment numbers:

- **Automotive**: +38% year-over-year to a record **$1.33 billion**
- **IoT**: +9% to **$1.73 billion**
- **Handsets (QCT)**: [Premium tier held up while lower tiers weakened](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-qualcomms-q2-2026-beats-expectations-stock-rises-93CH-4647253), per CFO Akash Palkhiwala

[Gross margin: 53.8%](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/QCOM/10-q-qualcomm-inc-de-quarterly-earnings-report-3c0b90ab8861.html), per the company's 10-Q. [The company said it expects a significant increase in new smart-glasses launches starting in the second half of 2026](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-qualcomms-q2-2026-beats-expectations-stock-rises-93CH-4647253), a category CEO Cristiano Amon said would "catalyze an inflection point in customer demand."

## The OpenAI Angle

On April 27, [Qualcomm shares jumped roughly 7%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/27/qualcomm-qcom-openai-smartphone-chip-partnership-stock.html) after supply-chain analyst Ming-Chi Kuo reported that [OpenAI was working with Qualcomm and MediaTek](https://www.forbes.com/sites/tylerroush/2026/04/27/could-this-new-ai-smartphone-compete-with-apple-qualcomm-shares-rise/) on a custom chip for an AI-native smartphone, with [Luxshare reportedly manufacturing the device](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-qualcomm-ai-phone-agents-replace-apps) — a claim that, as of this writing, [none of the three companies has confirmed](https://www.forbes.com/sites/tylerroush/2026/04/27/could-this-new-ai-smartphone-compete-with-apple-qualcomm-shares-rise/). This chip project is separate from OpenAI's other hardware effort with Jony Ive's io company; Forbes reported there is "no evidence Ive is involved in the Qualcomm effort." Mass production is currently targeting 2028.

Qualcomm being named as a silicon partner alongside MediaTek confirmed what its CEO had been hinting at for months.

## The CEO's Bigger Claim

In a series of [Fortune interviews in early May](https://fortune.com/2026/05/09/qualcomm-smartphone-ai-secret-device-openai-meta-wearables-agents/), CEO **Cristiano Amon** was unusually candid about the scope of Qualcomm's device pipeline. When asked which AI companies Qualcomm is working with on next-generation hardware, his answer:

> *"Pretty much all of them."*

He acknowledged ["secret form factors I cannot tell you about"](https://fortune.com/2026/05/09/qualcomm-smartphone-ai-secret-device-openai-meta-wearables-agents/) — wearable devices that he described not as smartphones or smartwatches, but as **things you wear**: glasses, jewelry, pins, pendants. The design logic is what he calls the **"ecosystem of you"**: [glasses positioned close to your eyes, ears, and mouth, feeding an AI agent the context it needs to act on your behalf](https://fortune.com/2026/05/09/qualcomm-smartphone-ai-secret-device-openai-meta-wearables-agents/). Amon named [OpenAI and Meta](https://fortune.com/2026/05/09/qualcomm-smartphone-ai-secret-device-openai-meta-wearables-agents/) among the partners building toward this vision, while declining to name others.

Amon's stated thesis: [2026 is the year of AI agents](https://fortune.com/2026/05/10/titans-and-disruptors-of-industry-qualcomm-ceo-cristiano-amon-ai-wearable-glasses-chips-6g/). [By 2027-2028, he expects meaningful workloads to start shifting from smartphones to these new AI-first form factors](https://fortune.com/2026/05/10/titans-and-disruptors-of-industry-qualcomm-ceo-cristiano-amon-ai-wearable-glasses-chips-6g/). He frames Qualcomm's position as hardware neutral ground — working across this transition without being tied to any single platform.

## Edge AI vs. Cloud AI: The Structural Argument

Why does running AI on-device matter? The cloud-vs-edge tradeoff has three dimensions:

**Latency**: A cloud request introduces round-trip time that on-device inference eliminates. For glasses that need to identify what you're looking at in real time, or earbuds that need to translate speech before you finish a sentence, network delay is unacceptable.

**Privacy**: Data processed locally never leaves the device. For personal health data, ambient audio, or financial queries, this is a genuine differentiator — not marketing. Several enterprise customers, particularly in healthcare and legal, will not use cloud-dependent AI for specific workflows.

**Cost and connectivity independence**: Every cloud AI call has a per-token cost. At scale — billions of smartphones, millions of vehicles, wearables worn 16 hours a day — on-device inference eliminates an operational expense that would otherwise compound continuously. Off-grid use cases (automotive, rural, aviation) also require local inference.

[The Snapdragon X2 Plus (announced at CES 2026) brings 80 TOPS of on-device NPU performance](https://www.engadget.com/computing/qualcomm-unveils-snapdragon-x2-plus-chip-at-ces-170000392.html) to mainstream AI PCs — not just the premium tier. [The Snapdragon Wear Elite extends the architecture to watches, pins, and pendants](https://mobilesyrup.com/2026/03/02/qualcomm-snapdragon-wear-elite/). Each category runs models locally that would have required a data center call two years ago.

## The Apple Modem Loss and the Bull Case Anyway

Qualcomm has faced one significant headwind that analysts cite regularly: [Apple began shipping its own in-house cellular modem in the iPhone Air](https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/02/21/apple-ends-its-qualcomm-dependency-with-the-new-c1-modem-chip), part of the iPhone 17 generation — though the [flagship iPhone 17 and iPhone 17 Pro still ship with Qualcomm's Snapdragon X80 modem](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/09/19/iphone-17-pro-max-qualcomm-snapdragon-x80/). The two companies' existing supply agreement runs through March 2027, and [analysts estimate the fuller revenue impact of Apple's in-house modem transition — worth an estimated $7 billion annually to Qualcomm — will land closer to 2028](https://futurumgroup.com/press-release/why-apples-7b-modem-exit-wont-damage-qualcomm-report-summary/), when Apple is expected to extend its own modem to the flagship iPhone 18 Pro line.

The stock's 75% surge in a month suggests the market has repriced around the idea that the automotive + AI device expansion more than compensates. [At $214 on May 22 — market cap approximately $225 billion, trailing P/E around 22, dividend yield 1.8%](https://www.foreignpolicyjournal.com/2026/05/22/qualcomm-inc-nasdaq-qcom-stock-hits-214-as-on-device-ai-becomes-smartphone-battleground/) — the valuation isn't cheap, but it isn't pricing in a dominance scenario either.

Analysts are divided on how high this goes. [Tigress Financial Partners' Ivan Feinseth raised his price target to $280 with a buy rating](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/tigress-financial-raises-qualcomm-price-target-to-280-from-270-buy-rating-maintained-ce7f5bdbdd8cf326) on May 9; [Daiwa's Louis Miscioscia upgraded the stock to Outperform with a $225 target](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/8847016/qcom-upgraded-by-daiwa-capital-price-target-raised-to-225) on May 8; other houses remain at hold, citing the Apple transition's lagging revenue effects and potential MediaTek competition in the mid-tier smartphone market.

## What Qualcomm Is and Isn't

Qualcomm is not an AI lab. It doesn't train frontier models, doesn't have a cloud platform, and isn't trying to be. What it has built — over decades — is a deep stack of power-efficient silicon, radio hardware, and driver software that runs on a significant fraction of the world's connected devices.

That stack now has a new job: running AI models locally, at scale, without a data center. The companies building the most-discussed AI devices of 2028 and beyond — OpenAI's AI-native smartphone, [Meta's next generation of Ray-Ban smart glasses](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/23/meta-glasses-are-new-smart-glasses-starting-at-299.html), the unnamed secret devices Amon is building with partners he won't identify — are converging on Qualcomm's hardware because no one else has both the NPU performance and the power envelope required for always-on wearable AI.

[Nvidia dominates the AI data center](https://siliconanalysts.com/analysis/nvidia-ai-accelerator-market-share-2024-2026), commanding roughly 80% of AI accelerator shipments. Apple dominates its own ecosystem. Qualcomm is quietly becoming the infrastructure layer for everything in between — the edge AI devices that will be the surface area through which most people experience AI agents in daily life.

The market is starting to notice.
